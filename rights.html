Know what you can say no to.

Plain-language answers about restaurant, hospital and shop bills in India — with the source, the date, and an honest note when things are unclear.

According to guidelines issued by the Central Consumer Protection Authority (CCPA) in July 2022, hotels and restaurants should not add a service charge to the bill automatically or by default, and should not collect it under any other name. Paying it is voluntary and at the consumer's discretion.

The guidelines also say service charge should not be added to the food bill with GST then charged on the total. These guidelines were challenged in court; check the latest position before relying on them.

Yes — under the 2022 guidelines you may request its removal. Be polite and specific: point to the line on the bill and say you'd like to pay without it. If you were happy with the service, you're free to leave a tip instead.

If the restaurant refuses, note the time, the staff member's response, and keep the bill. You can then contact the National Consumer Helpline.

For a sale within the same state, GST is split equally between the Centre (CGST) and the State (SGST). So a 5% GST appears as 2.5% CGST + 2.5% SGST. That's one tax shown in two halves — not double taxation.

For inter-state supplies you'd normally see IGST instead. PayRight checks that the two halves are equal and calculated on the right amount.

Health care services by clinical establishments are generally exempt from GST. Some items can still attract GST — for example, non-ICU room rent above ₹5,000 per day (since July 2022), or medicines sold from a pharmacy. If a hospital bill shows GST, ask which items it applies to.

Hospitals count days differently — some by night, some by calendar day or by a fixed check-out time. PayRight compares per-day charges with the admission and discharge dates printed on the bill and flags a mismatch for you to ask about. It's a question, not an accusation.

Medicines are packaged goods with a printed MRP, and selling above MRP is generally not allowed under the Legal Metrology rules. PayRight checks every medicine line that shows an MRP.

For pre-packaged goods sold at retail, the Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules, 2011 require the maximum retail price to be declared, and selling above it is generally not allowed.

Restaurants and hotels are a known exception for items served as part of the dining experience (e.g. bottled water at a table), following a 2017 Supreme Court ruling. The context matters.

Before you leave the table

Keep these six things.

  1. The original printed bill — not just a payment SMS
  2. A clear photo of the bill, all edges visible
  3. The menu price or offer you were shown
  4. Your payment proof — UPI, card or cash receipt
  5. Date, time and branch of the visit
  6. A note of who you spoke to and what they said
